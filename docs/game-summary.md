# SKETCHY — Game Summary

SKETCHY is a one-button community platformer played directly inside Reddit. Players guide an animated pencil through obstacle courses, reach a pencil sharpener at the finish, and then get the chance to change the course for everyone who follows. Its central hook is simple: **beat the level, leave your curse, and prove you can beat it again.**

The game takes place in a playful notebook world, with scribbled scenery, paper stacks, rulers, erasers, staplers, and other desk objects forming the terrain and hazards. The pencil emerges from a pencil case at the start and dives into a sharpener when it finishes. Expressive animation, sound effects, and quick retries give the game a mischievous tone.

Once a run starts, the pencil moves forward automatically. Players tap or click to jump, or use Space, W, or the Up arrow. Holding the button produces a higher jump; releasing it early makes a shorter hop. Success comes from learning the course and timing jumps around gaps, platforms, and traps. Death sends the player back to the start for another attempt.

The main play loop is:

1. **Play a course.** Learn its layout and survive the obstacles left by its creator and other players.
2. **Reach the finish.** Clear the level and record the run.
3. **Leave a curse.** Add one object to the course, usually a trap intended to catch the next player.
4. **Prove it.** Complete the modified course before publishing the change.
5. **See what happens.** Return to find out who your contribution caught and who made it through.

Cursing is the main social mechanic, but players can also choose Next Level after a clear without adding a curse. The curse editor supports hazards, platforms, and power-ups. It also allows an optional removal of an eligible trap or platform, and an extension of the course when space permits. Every published change requires a successful verification run of the modified layout.

Hazards include staplers, stationary and moving gears, chargers, floaters, spikes, ceiling spikes, spike mines, zappers, and swinging maces. Power-ups give players additional ways to survive:

- **Shield:** absorbs a hazard hit.
- **Speed Boost:** temporarily increases running speed.
- **Wings:** grants one extra mid-air jump.
- **Stopwatch:** temporarily slows affected moving hazards and platforms while leaving the player and run timer unchanged.

Players can also build original courses in a grid-based level editor using terrain, platforms, hazards, power-ups, a spawn point, and a finish. They must beat their creation before publishing it. Published courses can appear as their own subreddit posts, where the community can play and continue modifying them.

Discovery includes Trending, Deadliest, Speedrun, and New browsing options, plus a featured Level of the Day. Course statistics show attempts, clears, completion rate, and difficulty. A tutorial introduces the controls and curse mechanic, while the protected starter course, First Blood, gives new players a gentler introduction and cannot be cursed.

Progression centers on improving at courses, creating levels, and earning a reputation through effective sabotage. Player traps carry their creator's identity, so a death can show whose trap caused it. The Top Cursers leaderboards rank players by trap kills, both globally and for individual levels. Personal stats also show how many players each curse caught and how many passed it, including new activity since the player's last visit.

Contribution limits help keep courses shared: each player can publish up to 10 levels and leave up to 30 curses per day, with a maximum of three curses on any one level. Someone else must contribute between a player's successive curses on the same course. New sabotage traps cannot be placed in the first 10 columns, preserving room at the start.

SKETCHY's community supplies both the challenges and the reasons to return: a course can change after you beat it, another player's trap can become your next obstacle, and your own additions can keep catching players while you are away.
